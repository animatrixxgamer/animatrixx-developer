# Animatrixx Developer — Positioning

_Solo developer business with agency standards._
Working copy: reuse these lines on the site, in bios, and in client messages. Numbers and proof points are real; keep them that way.

---

## 1. The core statement

> **Animatrixx Developer is a one-person software studio that works like a small agency: written scope before money, a live preview before launch, and no feature promised until it has been tested.**

The thing being sold is not "a website". It is **a developer who finishes what he starts and tells you the truth about it**. That is the whole position. Everything below is that sentence in different lengths.

## 3. Why "agency standards" and not "cheap websites"

Cheap-website sellers compete on price and disappear after delivery. Full agencies are slow and expensive for a salon with a ₦40,000 budget. The gap between them is **a small business that wants to be treated seriously on a small budget**.

That is the position. You are not the cheapest, and you must never claim to be the best. You are the one who:

- puts the scope and the price in writing before taking money;
- shows a working preview instead of describing one;
- says "not built yet" about anything not built yet;
- is reachable afterwards, and doesn't vanish when a link breaks.

Say that plainly and it beats any amount of "we are passionate about digital transformation".

## 5. Client messages

### 5a. First approach (WhatsApp or DM) — small business

> Good day. My name is [name], a developer in Ondo State — Animatrixx Developer.
>
> I build simple, mobile-friendly websites for small businesses. For [business name] that would mean one page with your services, your prices, your location, and a WhatsApp button so customers can message you in one tap.
>
> I can show you a working example before you decide anything, and the price is agreed in writing first.
>
> Would you like me to send you the sample? No obligation.

**Why it works:** it names the business, says exactly what they'd get, offers proof, and removes the risk of replying. It does not ask "do you need a website?" — the answer to that is usually no.

### 5b. First approach — school or church

> Good day, sir/ma. I'm [name] from Animatrixx Developer in Ondo.
>
> I recently built a school website and management platform — public pages for admissions and news, plus a staff dashboard for fees, attendance and results, and a portal where parents check their child's record. It's live and I can send the link.
>
> If [school name] is considering something similar, I can start with just the public website and add the management side later, so the cost is staged.
>
> Happy to show you what's working before any discussion of price.

**Why it works:** the staged offer lowers the commitment and shows you understand a school can't buy everything at once.

### 5c. "How much?"

> It depends on scope, and I'd rather quote accurately than guess. For a one-page site it starts at ₦35,000; a five-page business site from ₦60,000; a Python automation script from ₦15,000. Maintenance is ₦10,000–₦20,000 a month.
>
> If you tell me what the business does and what the site needs to achieve, I'll send a fixed quote in writing — the price and the date, in one message, before any payment.

### 5d. "Can you build [something much bigger]?"

> Honestly, no — not yet, and not on my own. That needs [security review / a support team / payment compliance] and I'd be doing you a disservice pretending otherwise.
>
> What I can do is [the smaller piece I can actually deliver well]. If you want the full system, I'd rather point you to a team that does it properly than take your money and struggle.

**This reply wins more work than it loses.** People remember the developer who told them no.

### 5e. The scope confirmation (send before any deposit)

> **Project:** [one line]
> **Deliverables:** [three to five bullets, concrete]
> **Not included:** [the things people assume — e.g. hosting fees, copywriting, logo design]
> **Price:** ₦[amount], with ₦[deposit] to start and the balance on delivery.
> **Timeline:** preview by [date], live by [date].
> **Revisions:** one round of changes within scope included; anything new is quoted separately.
> **Payment:** [guardian-supervised account details]
>
> Reply "agreed" and I'll start.

### 5f. Follow-up (once, politely, after 5–7 days)

> Good day. Just following up on my message about a website for [business]. If now isn't the right time, no problem at all — I'm around whenever it becomes useful. Here's the sample again: [link]

One follow-up. Then stop.

## 6. What not to say

| Don't | Say instead |
|---|---|
| "I'm a professional agency with years of experience" | "I'm a developer building this properly, and here's what I've shipped." |
| "We can build anything you need" | "Here's what I build well, and what I don't take on yet." |
| "100% satisfaction guaranteed" | "One round of revisions included; anything beyond the scope is quoted." |
| "Unlimited changes" | Never. It is how small jobs become unpaid jobs. |
| "It's fully secure and production-ready" | "It handles X correctly; it hasn't had a professional security review." |
| Prices with no scope | Price + deliverables + date, in writing, together. |

---

## 7. Operating rules that make the position real

1. **Written scope before money.** Every time, even for ₦15,000.
2. **Deposit on anything above a one-page site.** 50% is normal.
3. **Preview before launch.** The client clicks it before they pay the balance.
4. **Test on a phone, on mobile data.** Most of your clients will only ever see it there.
5. **Never spend a deposit as income.** It buys the work, not your data bundle.
6. **Say "not built yet" out loud.** It is cheaper than being caught.
7. **One follow-up per prospect.** Beyond that it stops being business.
8. **Guardian-supervised payments** until you're 18 — state it plainly if a client asks; it reads as careful, not childish.

## 8. The one-sentence version to memorise

If you can say that without hesitating, you can hold a client conversation. The portfolio only has to prove it.
